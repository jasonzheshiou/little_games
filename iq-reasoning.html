Abstract

Expert-tier pattern reasoning. Every board is generated from a hidden rule set, so there is exactly one tile that satisfies it — and the wrong tiles differ from the right one in a single attribute. Three families: matrix rule induction, figural analogies, and invariant spotting. Six difficulties, from Warm-up (free hints) to Oblivion (one wrong tile closes the board, no skip, 75-second clock). Hints cost points; so does hesitation.

Abstract

Drag the one tile that obeys every hidden rule and drop it on the dashed slot. Tiles can also be focused with Tab and placed with Enter.

How the three puzzle families work
  • Matrix rule induction — one panel of the 3×3 matrix is missing. Up to six attributes (shape, count, rotation, size, fill style, secondary glyph) are each governed by their own hidden rule that depends on row and column position. Distractors break exactly one rule.
  • Figural analogy — A is related to B by one or two hidden transformations. The identical transformation must be applied to C.
  • Odd one out — four tiles share a hidden invariant; one tile violates it. The invariant is usually arithmetic, not visual.
  • Difficulty changes what the generator is allowed to do, not just the score. Warm-up plays two or three attributes with blunt rules and free hints; Nightmare always runs all six attributes with the subtlest rules, six near-identical tiles, and no hints at all.
  • Abyss and Oblivion unlock a second rule tier — patterns that depend on the centre panel or advance at different rates along rows and columns — and add real pressure: a minimum number of rules that must genuinely vary, a hard attempt limit (Oblivion allows one guess), a per-board countdown, and no skip on Oblivion.
